# Model metrics

Generated by `python -m src.train`. 1,180 listings, 43 kecamatan groups, 5-fold CV, seed 42. Target `log(price_idr)`; metrics on the Rupiah scale (R² on log scale). Values are mean ± standard deviation across folds.

## Comparison

| Model | Spatial MAPE | Spatial MdAPE | Spatial MAE (Rp) | Spatial R² (log) | Random MAPE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B0 baseline (median price/m² × land) | 46.1 ± 6.7% | 36.3 ± 1.9% | Rp 751 jt ± Rp 331 jt | 0.53 ± 0.08 | 42.1 ± 2.1% |
| M1 Ridge alpha=10 | 28.9 ± 2.7% | 21.4 ± 1.2% | Rp 490 jt ± Rp 165 jt | 0.80 ± 0.03 | 27.2 ± 1.6% |
| M2 LightGBM leaves=7 min_child=10 | 25.6 ± 3.2% | 19.5 ± 3.8% | Rp 487 jt ± Rp 191 jt | 0.81 ± 0.03 | 24.2 ± 1.3% |

**Spatial CV** = GroupKFold by kecamatan (`kecamatan_code`): test kecamatan are unseen in training. **Random CV** = shuffled KFold. The same folds are used for every model.

## Spatial vs random CV

Under spatial CV the model is always tested on kecamatan it has never seen — the honest setting for an app that must price houses in thinly covered areas. LightGBM's error rises from 24.2% (random CV) to 25.6% (spatial CV), a gap of 1.4 points. The gap is small. Two likely reasons: cleaning removed reposts of the same house, so near-identical listings rarely sit on both sides of a split; and the location features describe a kecamatan (distances, amenity density) rather than identify it, so they carry over to unseen kecamatan. Random CV is still the optimistic number, because neighbors from the same kecamatan help it. LightGBM beats the baseline under spatial CV (25.6% vs 46.1% MAPE), so house size, rooms and location features add real information beyond 'median price per m² in the kabupaten'. MAPE is pulled up by a few large misses (several are data errors — see worst predictions); the median error (MdAPE) is the better guide to a typical listing.

## Known limitations

- **Point estimate is a typical price, not an average.** The models predict `log(price)` and the app converts back with `exp()`, with no smearing correction. That gives roughly the median price for a house like this one, which sits below the mean when errors are skewed. It is one reason the most expensive houses (> 5 M) are under-predicted. P6's quantile models report the spread around it.
- **Few listings outside Sleman.** Gunungkidul and Kulon Progo have very few listings, so their error estimates above are noisy.
- **Asking prices, not sale prices,** from one site in March–May 2024.

## Price range (quantile models)

LightGBM quantile models at 0.1, 0.5, 0.9 with the point model's tree settings (`num_leaves=7`, `min_child_samples=10`). The app shows [q10, q90] as the likely range (nominally 80%). Crossing quantiles are sorted per row (16 of 1,180 rows crossed under spatial CV).

| CV | Coverage of [q10, q90] | Below q10 | Above q90 | Width (q90 − q10) / q50 | Point estimate outside range |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| spatial | 80.1 ± 6.9% | 8.3% | 11.6% | 88% (median 82%) | 0.3% |
| random | 81.5 ± 3.6% | 8.7% | 9.7% | 83% (median 79%) | 0.5% |

Spatial coverage is **80.1%**, inside the 75–85% target. Width is the range as a share of the middle (q50) estimate. The coverage above is after calibration; the factor was chosen on these same out-of-fold predictions, so the nested check below is the honest number.

Calibration factor applied: **1.32** (log interval widened around q50; raw quantiles covered 68.7% spatial / 69.3% random). A factor of 1.32 would reach 80% spatial coverage; chosen on 4 folds and tested on the 5th it gives 80.2 ± 8.3% coverage.

### Per-kabupaten range calibration (P7b U2)

Each kabupaten with ≥ 40 rows gets the smallest scale on the log half-widths around q50 that reaches 80% spatial out-of-fold coverage, clipped to 1–2; smaller kabupaten keep the global factor 1.32. "Before" = global factor for every row; "raw" = no widening. Scales are stored in `models/metadata.json` (`range_scales`) and applied by `predict_house`.

| Kabupaten | Rows | Scale | Coverage raw | Coverage before | Coverage after | Honest (nested) | Mean width before | Mean width after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kota Yogyakarta | 122 | 1.63 | 62.3% | 75.4% | 80.3% | 81.1% | 104% | 134% |
| Sleman | 854 | 1.32 | 69.3% | 80.4% | 80.4% | 79.9% | 90% | 90% |
| Bantul | 172 | 1.32 | 69.2% | 80.2% | 80.2% | 77.9% | 77% | 77% |
| Kulon Progo | 26 | 1.32 (global) | 80.8% | 96.2% | 96.2% | 96.2% | 53% | 53% |
| Gunungkidul | 6 | 1.32 (global) | 50.0% | 50.0% | 50.0% | 50.0% | 64% | 64% |

Overall spatial coverage after: **80.6%** (fold mean 80.6 ± 7.2%), mean width 91% (median 83%).

**"Coverage after" is optimistic:** it is measured on the same out-of-fold predictions used to pick the scales. The "Honest (nested)" column chooses the global factor and the scales on 4 folds and measures the 5th: 79.9 ± 9.8% overall. The drift check (U4) on new listings is the independent test.

## Ablation: road km feature (P7b U1)

`road_km` / `has_road_km` parsed from listing text ("Jl. Kaliurang km 8", "Jakal km 7") for 109 of 1,180 listings (9.2%). Same grid, folds and selection rule as above; best model per family by spatial MAPE within each variant. Written by `python -m src.ablation_road_km`; parsing details in `reports/ablation_road_km.md`.

| Variant | Model | Spatial MAPE | Spatial MdAPE | Spatial MAE | Random MAPE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| without road km | Ridge alpha=10 | 28.91% ± 2.7 | 21.37% | Rp 490 jt | 27.24% |
| without road km | LightGBM leaves=7 min_child=10 | 25.62% ± 3.2 | 19.46% | Rp 487 jt | 24.23% |
| with road km | Ridge alpha=10 | 28.83% ± 2.6 | 20.95% | Rp 490 jt | 27.20% |
| with road km | LightGBM leaves=7 min_child=10 | 25.62% ± 3.2 | 18.97% | Rp 487 jt | 24.31% |

Rule (guide P7b U1): keep only if spatial-CV MAPE is equal or better. LightGBM: 25.62% without vs 25.62% with (fold std ± 3.2); MdAPE 19.46% → 18.97%; random MAPE 24.23% → 24.31%; on the listings with a marker 22.3% → 21.8%. Every difference is far inside the fold-to-fold spread.

**Decision: not kept** (`config.USE_ROAD_KM_FEATURES = False`). No measurable gain, and keeping it would add a road + km input to the app that helps under 10% of houses. The parser stays; `road_name`, `road_km`, `has_road_km` remain in `model_table.csv` for EDA.
